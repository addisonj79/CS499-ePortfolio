Introduction

Throughout my Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed skills in programming, software development, databases, algorithms, testing, security, and problem-solving. When I started the program, I focused mainly on writing code and getting programs to work correctly. As I progressed through my classes, I realized software development involves much more than that. Code needs to be organized, maintainable, secure, and designed to meet the needs of its users.

My coursework has helped me improve my ability to analyze problems, develop solutions, debug code, and evaluate different approaches. I have also learned the importance of testing, documentation, communication, and making thoughtful technical decisions. These skills have helped me understand how computer science concepts are applied to real software development challenges.

My CS 499 ePortfolio brings together work from my Computer Science program and demonstrates the skills I have developed. My portfolio includes my CS 300 Course Planner and my CS 360 mobile event-tracking application. These projects demonstrate different areas of computer science and provide examples of how I have applied programming, algorithms, software design, and data management concepts.

Skills and Growth

One of the most important areas of growth for me has been programming. I have developed my ability to break problems into smaller steps, select appropriate approaches, write code, and troubleshoot errors. Working with languages such as C++ and other programming tools has helped me understand that different problems require different solutions.

I have also learned more about algorithms and data structures. My CS 300 Course Planner project helped me understand how a binary search tree can organize information and how in-order traversal can display course information in sorted order. This experience helped me see how the choice of a data structure affects the way a program processes and retrieves information.

Another important area of growth has been software engineering and design. My CS 360 mobile event-tracking application helped me consider how application functionality, data management, input validation, and usability work together. I have learned that a program should not only produce the correct results but should also be understandable, maintainable, and useful to the people who interact with it.

My coursework has also helped me recognize the importance of security. Developers need to consider how information is protected, how systems could be misused, and how design decisions can affect privacy and reliability. I plan to continue building these skills as I gain more experience in software development.

Computer Science Program Course Outcomes

The five course outcomes for my Computer Science program represent the technical and professional skills I have developed throughout my coursework. They address collaboration, communication, algorithmic problem-solving, software development, and security. My ePortfolio connects these outcomes to projects and experiences that demonstrate my understanding of computer science concepts and their practical applications.

Outcome 1: Collaboration and Decision-Making

Employ strategies for building collaborative environments that enable diverse audiences to support organizational decision-making in the field of computer science.

This outcome emphasizes the importance of working with others, considering different perspectives, and communicating information to support informed decisions. Software development often involves people with different responsibilities and levels of technical knowledge. Developers need to understand project requirements, explain their reasoning, and consider feedback when evaluating possible solutions.

Through my coursework, I have learned that technical decisions should be based on the problem being solved, the needs of users, and the goals of a project. My CS 300 Course Planner and CS 360 mobile event-tracking application required me to consider the purpose of the software and how its functionality supports that purpose. These experiences reinforced the importance of considering requirements and the people who will use or maintain a program.

As I continue developing my professional skills, I want to build on this foundation by listening to feedback, explaining technical decisions clearly, and working effectively with people who have different perspectives. These practices can help me contribute to collaborative development environments and support better decisions.

Outcome 2: Professional Communication

Design, develop, and deliver professional-quality oral, written, and visual communications that are coherent, technically sound, and appropriately adapted to specific audiences and contexts.

This outcome focuses on communicating technical information clearly, accurately, and professionally. Computer scientists need to explain their work through written documentation, presentations, diagrams, code comments, and discussions. Effective communication requires organizing information and adapting the explanation to the intended audience.

Creating this ePortfolio has given me an opportunity to organize my work and explain the purpose of my projects, the concepts they demonstrate, and the skills I have gained. For example, I can explain how my CS 300 Course Planner uses a binary search tree and in-order traversal to organize and display course information. I can also describe the purpose of my CS 360 application and discuss the software development concepts involved.

This experience has helped me understand that technical communication should explain not only what a program does, but also how it works and why particular decisions matter. I plan to apply these skills when documenting code, explaining solutions, communicating with teammates, and presenting technical information to different audiences.

Outcome 3: Algorithms, Data Structures, and Problem-Solving

Design and evaluate computing solutions that solve a given problem using algorithmic principles and computer science practices and standards appropriate to its solution while managing the trade-offs involved in design choices.

This outcome emphasizes selecting appropriate algorithms and data structures, evaluating possible solutions, and understanding the trade-offs involved in design decisions. A good solution should meet the requirements of a problem while considering factors such as efficiency, organization, readability, and maintainability.

My CS 300 Course Planner demonstrates this outcome through its use of C++ and a binary search tree to organize course information. The project also used in-order traversal to display the information in sorted order. Working on this project helped me understand how a data structure can support program requirements and how the choice of structure affects the way information is processed and retrieved.

This project taught me to think about why one approach may be more appropriate than another instead of simply choosing the first solution that works. Different implementations can produce the same result while varying in efficiency, complexity, and maintainability. I can apply this knowledge by analyzing requirements, evaluating different approaches, testing solutions, and selecting algorithms and data structures that fit the problem.

Outcome 4: Software Development and Computing Practices

Demonstrate an ability to use well-founded and innovative techniques, skills, and tools in computing practices for the purpose of implementing computer solutions that deliver value and accomplish industry-specific goals.

This outcome focuses on applying programming knowledge, development tools, and established computing practices to create useful solutions. Developing software requires understanding the problem, selecting appropriate tools, implementing functionality, and considering whether the final solution meets the needs of its intended users.

My CS 360 mobile event-tracking application is an example of applying software development concepts to a practical problem. The application manages event information and provides functionality related to that information. Reviewing this project helped me consider the importance of application organization, usability, input validation, and maintainability when developing software.

My CS 300 Course Planner demonstrates another application of computing practices through its use of programming and data structures to organize course information. Together, these projects show how different computer science concepts can be applied to meet different requirements. I plan to continue using appropriate development tools, following coding standards, testing my work, and evaluating how my solutions provide value to users.

Outcome 5: Security and Protection of Data

Develop a security mindset that anticipates adversarial exploits in software architecture and designs to expose potential vulnerabilities, mitigate design flaws, and ensure privacy and enhanced security of data and resources.

This outcome emphasizes the importance of considering security throughout the software development process. Developers need to think about how a system could be misused, what vulnerabilities could expose information, and how design decisions can reduce those risks. Security should be considered during design and implementation rather than only after software has been completed.

My CS 305 secure communications coursework helped me develop a better understanding of protecting information and the importance of secure communication practices. This experience reinforced the need to consider how information is transmitted and protected, as well as how security decisions affect the reliability and trustworthiness of a system.

I can apply this security mindset when evaluating applications such as my CS 360 mobile event-tracking application. For example, developers should consider how application data is accessed, how user input is handled, and what could happen if information were exposed or improperly modified. Thinking through these possibilities helps identify areas that may need additional protection.

Moving forward, I want to continue improving my ability to identify vulnerabilities, evaluate risks, validate input, protect sensitive information, and follow secure coding practices. Developing these habits will help me create software that is functional, reliable, and responsible in how it handles data and resources.

Overall, these five outcomes reflect the technical and professional skills I have developed during my Computer Science program. Collaboration and communication help me explain ideas and work with others. Algorithms and software engineering help me develop solutions to practical problems, while security reminds me to consider risks and protect information. My ePortfolio brings these areas together and gives me a foundation to continue improving as a software developer.
