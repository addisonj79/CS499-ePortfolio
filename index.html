Introduction

Throughout my Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have learned a lot about programming, software development, databases, algorithms, and security. When I started the program, I focused mainly on writing code and getting programs to work correctly. As I progressed through my classes, I realized software development involves much more than that. Code also needs to be organized, tested, secure, and easy for other people to understand and maintain. Completing my CS 499 capstone has given me a chance to reflect on what I have learned and show how I can apply those skills to real programming projects.

One of the biggest skills I have developed is solving problems. Throughout my classes, I have worked through programming challenges, debugged errors, and learned to break problems into smaller steps. I have also learned the importance of communication and teamwork. Software developers need to understand requirements, explain their decisions, and consider the needs of the people who will use their applications. Even when working independently, it is important to write code that another developer can understand and maintain.

My coursework has also strengthened my understanding of data structures and algorithms. In my CS 300 Course Planner project, I worked with C++ and a binary search tree to organize and retrieve course information. This helped me understand how the right data structure affects how information is stored and accessed. I also learned about software engineering and databases through projects that involved managing information and improving application functionality. In CS 305, I studied secure communications and learned more about protecting information and why security needs to be considered in software development. These experiences have helped me build a foundation I can continue to develop throughout my career.

My Capstone and Portfolio

My CS 499 ePortfolio brings together projects that demonstrate different areas of my computer science knowledge. My CS 360 mobile event-tracking application focuses on application development and managing event information. Through my enhancement work, I looked at ways to improve the application's navigation, input validation, event-management features, and overall organization. This project helped me think more about the user experience and how improvements can make software easier to use and maintain.

My CS 300 Course Planner shows my understanding of data structures and algorithms. Working with a binary search tree helped me apply programming concepts to a practical problem and understand how to organize and retrieve information. My database-related work also demonstrates the importance of storing and managing information correctly. Although these projects focus on different areas, both show how computer science concepts can be applied to develop useful software.

My security coursework adds another part to my overall experience by showing the importance of protecting information and using secure development practices. Together, these projects demonstrate that I have gained experience in several areas of computer science rather than focusing on only one type of programming task. Developing my ePortfolio has also helped me recognize my progress and given me a way to present my skills to potential employers.

Computer Science Program Learning Outcomes

Throughout my Computer Science program, I have developed skills that connect to the program learning outcomes. My coursework and capstone have helped me understand how communication, problem solving, programming, and the use of computing tools work together when developing software.

Outcome 1: Collaboration

Throughout my Computer Science program, I have learned the importance of considering different perspectives and understanding user needs when developing software. While working on my CS 360 mobile event-tracking application, I considered how users would interact with the application and how improvements to navigation and input validation could make it easier to use. I have also learned that organizing code and documenting changes makes it easier for other developers to understand and maintain a project. These experiences have helped me understand how communication, organization, and considering user needs contribute to successful software development.

Outcome 2: Professional Communication

Throughout my Computer Science program, I have improved my ability to explain technical concepts and communicate my work clearly. Creating my CS 499 ePortfolio has given me an opportunity to describe my projects, explain the enhancements I made, and discuss the reasoning behind my technical decisions. For example, explaining my CS 300 Course Planner requires me to describe how a binary search tree organizes and retrieves course information in a way that others can understand. Developing my portfolio has also helped me practice presenting technical information in a professional manner. These skills will help me communicate effectively with other developers, stakeholders, and users in my future career.

Outcome 3: Computing Solutions and Algorithms

My CS 300 Course Planner demonstrates how I can apply data structures and algorithmic principles to a programming problem. Using a binary search tree helped me understand how course information can be organized and retrieved. My CS 360 mobile event-tracking application also required me to consider how software could be improved to better meet user needs. These projects helped me understand the importance of choosing appropriate solutions and considering efficiency, reliability, and maintainability.

Outcome 4: Computing Tools and Techniques

Throughout my degree, I have gained experience with programming languages, development tools, databases, testing, and debugging. My work with C++ and the Course Planner allowed me to apply programming concepts to a practical problem, while my mobile application work helped me better understand application functionality and data management. My CS 305 security coursework also taught me why it is important to use appropriate techniques to protect information. These experiences have prepared me to continue learning new technologies and applying them to future projects.

Career Goals and Conclusion

My goal is to pursue a career in software development where I can use my programming and problem-solving skills to build useful applications. I know I will keep learning after graduation, especially as I gain experience on real-world projects. I want to improve my technical abilities, become more comfortable working with larger systems, and learn from other developers.

Overall, my time at SNHU has helped me become more confident in my ability to analyze problems, develop solutions, and improve existing software. My capstone ePortfolio demonstrates the knowledge I have gained in programming, algorithms, databases, software engineering, and security. It has also helped me recognize the value of communication, testing, and writing maintainable code. I am proud of the progress I have made throughout my degree, and I look forward to building on these skills as I begin my career in computer science.
